The honest answer

Is it worth 80,000 taka?

The return flight (~43k) is more than half the money, and you pay it whatever you do once you're there. Each riding day after that costs only about 6–7k. So whether the trip is worth it comes down to how good those riding days are.

Worth it with Option B

The Kali Gandaki up to Muktinath: Rupse Falls, a gorge between Dhaulagiri and Annapurna, Marpha's apple lanes, the medieval alleys of Kagbeni, and the 3,710 m temple. Nothing in Bangladesh, and very little in the region, looks like this from a bike seat.

Weaker with Option A

Pokhara day rides are pretty: lakes, hill towns and big views of Machhapuchhre. But a lot of it is tarmac and town traffic. It's a nice holiday, but not much of an 80k adventure.

Same price either way

Both options come out at ~80k (see the budget). Teahouse rooms in Mustang cost about the same as a shared Pokhara room, so B gives you far more for the same money.

Pick one

Option A: come back each night. Option B: ditch the hotel.

Option A

Pokhara base

One Lakeside room for 4 nights. Ride out light every morning and come back for dinner and the lakeside bars.

  • No luggage on the bikes, and you can leave things in the room
  • Flexible: easy to drop a day if Nabil's dates change
  • Paragliding and Lakeside evenings fit in easily
  • Mostly tarmac, so less of a real Xpulse workout
  • No Muktinath, no Mustang, no 8,000 m peaks up close
Fun, easy, safe. A 7/10.
Option B · recommended

The Mustang run

Check out of Pokhara with a small bag strapped on, and stay in a different teahouse each night as you ride up the Kali Gandaki.

  • Hot springs, then a forest under Dhaulagiri, then a medieval mud-brick village
  • Gravel, river crossings, switchbacks: exactly what the Xpulse is built for
  • Muktinath at 3,710 m, and the edge of the forbidden Kingdom of Lo
  • A long ride back down on the last day (~8 h)
  • Basic teahouses: thin walls, cold nights, shared bathrooms
The 80k-worthy version. A 10/10.

Bandipur: a Newari hill town with no cars

~150 km round trip · Prithvi Highway + switchbacks

Place by place, south → north

What you'll see, feel and learn

Pokhara base

820 m · both options
What's there
A lake town with the Annapurna range straight above it. Lakeside cafes, rental shops and gear shops.
Do
Sarangkot sunrise, a Phewa boat to Tal Barahi, the Peace Pagoda, Davis Falls and Gupteshwor Cave, paragliding.
USP
Probably the only city in the world where you can look at a 7,000 m peak from your breakfast table.

Begnas & Rupa solo day

~15 km east of Lakeside
What's there
Two quiet lakes with a ridge road between them, fish farms and paddy fields.
Why
Phewa without the crowds, and an easy road to learn Nepali traffic on.

Tatopani & Rupse Falls must

1,190 m · ~110–125 km from Pokhara
What's there
Riverside natural hot springs, and a waterfall (often quoted as ~300 m) crashing down right beside the road.
Feel
Your first soak after a day of dust. A subtropical valley with oranges and bananas.
Challenge
Wet, slippery road under the falls' spray, and jeeps parked for photos.

The gorge: Ghasa → Lete must

2,000 → 2,500 m
What's there
The deepest gorge on Earth. Dhaulagiri on your left and Annapurna I on your right, both over 8,000 m.
Challenge
The hardest riding. One-lane cuttings, loose rock, water crossings, and buses that won't give way. Stand on the pegs.
Learn
Ghasa is where you cross from Hindu hill country into Thakali and Tibetan Buddhist culture.

Lete, Kalopani & Titi Lake gem

~2,530 m · Option B night 2
What's there
Pine forest and the best panorama on the route: Dhaulagiri's icefall, Nilgiri and Annapurna I. Titi Lake is a quiet forest lake a walk above the road.
Feel
Golden light on Nilgiri at sunset, then a clear, still, freezing morning.
USP
The last green place before the desert.

Tukuche gem

~2,590 m · old salt-trade capital
What's there
Once the richest Thakali town. Trader mansions with carved windows and courtyards, and fruit-brandy distilling.
Learn
How Tibetan salt traded for Indian grain built this whole valley.

Marpha must

~2,670 m · Nepal's apple capital
What's there
White-washed stone lanes, a cliffside gompa, orchards, apple brandy, cider and pie. Next door is Chhairo, a Tibetan refugee settlement with an old gompa.
Challenge
This is where the afternoon wind starts. Be past Jomsom before noon.

Thini & Dhumba Lake gem

~5 km from Jomsom · ~2,830 m
What's there
Thini is an old Thakali village. Dhumba is a sacred turquoise lake that mirrors Nilgiri.
Respect
It's holy to locals: no swimming, and don't touch the water.

Kagbeni must

2,810 m · Option B night 3
What's there
A medieval village of mud tunnels, the red 15th-century Kag Chode monastery, and clay protector figures at the village edges. The Upper Mustang border sign, which you can't pass without a ~$500 permit.
Feel
The edge of a forbidden kingdom. Yak burger at the "YacDonald's" joke cafe.

Jharkot gem

~3,500 m · on the way to Muktinath
What's there
A ruined hilltop fort with a Sakya monastery. Most riders blast past it.
Why
A 20-minute stop for the best view down the Muktinath valley and across to cliff-top Jhong.

Muktinath summit

3,710 m · the top of the trip
What's there
108 bull-headed spouts of freezing water that pilgrims run under, and Jwala Mai, a natural-gas flame burning beside water. Sacred to both Hindus and Buddhists. Thorong La (5,416 m) is just above.
Challenge
Altitude. Walk slowly, drink water, and go down if a headache gets worse. Don't sleep up here.
USP
The bike photo of the trip.
Context makes it hit harder

The story of this road

Older than the mountains

The Kali Gandaki was flowing before the Himalaya rose. It kept cutting down as the land pushed up, which is why it slices between two 8,000 m peaks.

The salt road

For centuries Tibetan salt and wool came down this valley and grain went back up. The Thakali traders who ran the route got rich, and their food, the Thakali thali, became Nepal's national comfort meal.

Sea fossils at 3,000 m

The black shaligram stones in the riverbed are ammonites from the floor of the ancient Tethys Sea. Hindus revere them as forms of Vishnu.

Kingdom of Lo

North of Kagbeni lay the kingdom of Mustang. It kept its own king until 2008 and is still restricted to visitors. Kagbeni is its front door.

Three faiths, one valley

Hindu Muktinath, Buddhist gompas everywhere, and in Lubra, Bön, the Himalaya's pre-Buddhist religion. Spin prayer wheels clockwise and keep chortens on your right.

Banana to desert in 48 h

Tatopani grows oranges. Two days later at Kagbeni it's a windswept high desert, and that change is a big part of the experience.

Get under 78k

  • Book the flight this week
  • Negotiate NPR 3,000/day for 2 bikes
  • Eat dinner where you sleep (teahouses often discount the room)
  • Skip paragliding

Cash

  • Carry USD, endorsed on your passport in BD. BDT is hard to change in Nepal.
  • Draw NPR in Pokhara and Beni. Jomsom ATMs are unreliable.
Pack

Prep checklist

Documents

  • Passport (6+ months valid) and a photocopy
  • BD driving licence with the motorcycle category
  • IDP from AAB: same day if you apply before noon, Sun–Thu. Bring passport and NID copies, your licence, 1 passport photo and 4 stamp-size photos.
  • 4 extra passport photos for ACAP/TIMS
  • Printed tickets, and endorsed USD

Gear

  • Riding gloves, a buff or mask, sunglasses
  • Warm layer + windproof shell (about 0 °C at dawn up high)
  • Your own helmet if you can carry it
  • Power bank (Nabil needs one too), phone mount, 2 bungees, cable ties
  • One small soft bag each for Option B
  • ORS, paracetamol, anti-diarrhoea pills, sunscreen, lip balm
  • Offline maps: Google Maps for Pokhara–Muktinath, plus Maps.me

The Ladakh question

Ladakh is not rideable in Feb–Mar: both roads into Leh are closed and it's −15 to −25 °C. The real window is June–September 2027, so you have 7–10 months to save, not 3. Don't pay a middleman upfront for an "India visa appointment in a month". Use the official IVAC portal, or an agent you can verify.
