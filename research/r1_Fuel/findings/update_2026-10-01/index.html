0. The ten-day picture in five lines

  1. There was no second hike and no duty cut. October pump prices are unchanged at diesel Tk 135, petrol Tk 160, octane Tk 165 and kerosene Tk 155. The government explicitly chose not to cut fuel taxes.
  2. The "October LC cliff" has been pushed back, not removed. On 29 Sep the Finance Division gave BPC a Tk 4,500 crore interest-free loan, the first in 11 years. That covers roughly 7–8 weeks of the diesel loss run-rate est..
  3. Pass-through is now visible and larger than the gazette. The gazetted bus fare rose 17 paisa/km (+6.7% metro). Realised fares on many trips rose Tk 5–20 in Dhaka and Tk 50–100 on long-distance routes. Truck charters rose ~19–40% per trip. Green chilli, eggplant, flour, eggs, milk and lentils all rose within a week.
  4. Furnace oil rose again (+Tk 11.92 to Tk 108.78/L from 1 Oct). Qatar extended LNG force majeure to Asian buyers through November. The power cost base is climbing again going into winter.
  5. Officials now say no further hike is planned. The State Minister said so on 23 Sep, and so did the BPC chairman on 29 Sep. Against that, the State Minister also said the full formula would put diesel at ~Tk 205, which leaves a gap of about Tk 70/L. It does not narrow it.

1. Prices, fiscal decisions, BPC financing, IFIs

1a. Pump prices for October: unchanged

  • On 30 Sep the Energy and Mineral Resources Division told the BPC chairman to keep the status quo for October. From 1 Oct: diesel Tk 135, octane Tk 165, petrol Tk 160, kerosene Tk 155. The stated aim is to give "some relief to the market and transport sectors." Source: DesheBideshe (Bangla), 30 Sep 2026, https://www.deshebideshe.com/456367/remaining-unchanged-in-october/
  • Corroborated by TBS, 30 Sep 2026, https://www.tbsnews.net/economy/energy/fuel-prices-remain-unchanged-october-1558911 (TBS says the announcement came via BERC, "with the approval of the competent authorities"), and bdnews24, 30 Sep 2026, https://bdnews24.com/business/f3ub8nrf1p
  • Discrepancy to note: TBS attributes the announcement to BERC, while DesheBideshe says the Energy Division instructed BPC. Both agree on the prices.

1b. Furnace oil: up for October

  • BERC set furnace oil at Tk 108.78/L from 00:00 on 1 Oct, up Tk 11.92 from Tk 96.86 (+12.3%). The price is based on average Platts from 29 Aug to 28 Sep, September crude CIF, and the exchange rate. Source: bdnews24, 30 Sep 2026, https://bdnews24.com/business/198sgq9tlw ; TBS, 30 Sep 2026, https://www.tbsnews.net/economy/energy/furnace-oil-price-rises-tk839-tk10878-amid-global-oil-volatility-1558606
    • Discrepancy: the TBS headline says "+Tk 8.39", but its body text and bdnews24 both say +Tk 11.92 from Tk 96.86. I use Tk 11.92.
    • TBS gives the path in between: Tk 113.54 was cut to Tk 109.10 at end-June, then cut further to Tk 96.86, then raised to Tk 108.78.
  • Jet A-1 for October: not confirmed.
  • LPG for October: BERC normally announces it in the first days of the month. Not yet published as of 1 Oct.

1c. Duty/VAT cut: considered and declined

  • The government declined a fuel tax cut. Fuel carries about 32% in cumulative duties and taxes, levied on invoice value, so revenue rises automatically with world prices. NBR fuel-tax collections rose ~90% in the first two months of FY27 (from ~Tk 2,000 crore to ~Tk 3,800 crore). TBS ties the refusal to the pay-scale cost (Tk 1.05 lakh crore over 17 months). Zahid Hussain: "The government has shifted from formula-based policy to decision-based policy." TBS reports that at least 55 countries used direct fiscal measures. Source: TBS, 23 Sep 2026, https://www.tbsnews.net/economy/energy/govt-shies-away-fuel-tax-cut-amid-pay-hike-1551121
  • Experts (M Shamsul Alam of CAB, Khondaker Golam Moazzem of CPD, Selim Raihan of SANEM) called for cutting fuel import taxes. The structure is 6% CD + 15% VAT + 2% AT + 2% AIT ≈ 25% on diesel imports. Tk 10,555 crore was collected from diesel, crude and petrol imports in Jan–Aug 2026, of which diesel was Tk 8,042 crore. Source: The Daily Star, 22 Sep 2026, https://www.thedailystar.net/news/bangladesh/news/experts-call-cutting-fuel-import-taxes-4279421
  • An op-ed puts taxes at ~Tk 38 of the claimed Tk 52/L diesel "subsidy" and proposes a fixed per-litre tax, citing Pakistan, Nepal and India, and Turkey's automatic stabiliser. Its Boro estimate is +3% cost per bigha, or ~7% in high-diesel areas, with no method given. Source: Daily Waadaa (op-ed), 24 Sep 2026, https://www.dailywaadaa.com/promissory-note/2026/09/24/a-regressive-inflationary-tax-masked-as-a-subsidy
  • Note: the tax burden estimates now differ by source: Tk 32.44/L in the original analysis, ~25% (Daily Star), ~32% (TBS) and ~Tk 38/L (Daily Waadaa). They are not measured on the same basis. P5 should use NBR's own per-litre figure.

1d. BPC financing and LC status

  • The Finance Division approved a Tk 4,500 crore interest-free loan to BPC, notified by letter on 29 Sep. Terms: repayment in half-yearly instalments over 5 years, including a 6-month grace period. It comes from the FY2025-26 operational-loan budget. It is the first such loan in 11 years. BPC had warned it could run out of LC funds in October. State Minister Anindya Islam Amit: diesel "would have cost about Tk 205 a litre if the automatic pricing mechanism had been fully applied." BPC also needs Tk 690 crore by December for the SPM loan, plus ITFC payments. Source: The Daily Star, 30 Sep 2026, https://www.thedailystar.net/news/bangladesh/news/after-11yrs-bpc-gets-tk-4500cr-loan-buy-fuel-4286591
  • Background: between 11 Mar and 8 Sep BPC sent 10 letters to the ministry; the last one asked for PMO attention. BPC Chairman Md Rafiqul Islam said opening LCs for October would be "difficult" without intervention. FY2025-26 fuel imports were a record $10.63 bn (+107%). Source: Ajker Patrika (Bangla), 21 Sep 2026, https://www.ajkerpatrika.com/business/ajpf8q18umzou
  • BPC chairman on 29 Sep: "we will try with our capacity so that prices never have to rise again." He said finances had been discussed with the Finance Ministry and were at a "satisfactory position", stocks were ~45 days, and procurement was complete through December. Source: Bangla TV (minor outlet, not corroborated by a major outlet in my searches), 29 Sep 2026, https://banglatv.tv/news/2026/09/110073/
  • Coverage est.: Tk 4,500 crore ÷ ~Tk 85 crore/day post-hike diesel loss (the impact_analysis estimate) ≈ 53 days. As a share of the Tk 15,000–20,000 crore extra working capital BPC says it needs: 22.5–30%. So the next BPC cash crunch plausibly falls in late November–December, the same window as Boro irrigation demand. This is an inference from the loan size and the loss run-rate, not a reported date.
  • Warning about a false lead: search engines return a widely syndicated story that BPC had "$670 m overdue as of 30 September" ($260 m ITFC, $410 m others). That story is from 3 Oct 2023 (TBS, https://www.tbsnews.net/bangladesh/energy/urgent-dollar-support-sought-fuel-import-711030). It is not 2026 data, so do not use it. I could not confirm any 2026 figure for overdue BPC dollar payments.

2. Pass-through already visible

2a. Bus fares (gazetted)

  • The Road Transport and Highways Division issued a notification on 22 Sep, published 23 Sep. It raises fares for diesel-run buses and minibuses by 17 paisa/km. Dhaka/Chattogram metro goes Tk 2.53 → 2.70/km, long-distance/inter-district Tk 2.23 → 2.40/km. CNG-run buses are unchanged. Owners had asked for 20 paisa. Sources: The Daily Star, 22 Sep 2026, https://www.thedailystar.net/news/transport/news/bus-fares-rise-17-paisa-km-amid-fuel-price-hike-4279791 ; Prothom Alo (Bangla), https://www.prothomalo.com/bangladesh/078dhpl08q ; TBS Bangla, https://www.tbsnews.net/bangla/bangladesh/news-details-542771
  • Minister Shaikh Rabiul Alam warned that overchargers face fines and could lose route permits. He said there would be no rail fare rise for now. Source: The Daily Star, 23 Sep 2026, https://www.thedailystar.net/news/transport/news/charge-passengers-extra-risk-losing-route-permits-rabiul-warns-bus-operators-4279826
  • Pass-through ratio est.: metro 17/253 = +6.7%, inter-district 17/223 = +7.6%, against diesel +17.4%. That gives 0.39–0.44, above the 0.30 assumed from April. The absolute metro rate (Tk 2.70) matches the impact analysis forecast (~Tk 2.69). The inter-district rate (Tk 2.40) beat the forecast (~Tk 2.35).
  • New minimum fares and a minibus per-km rate: not confirmed for this revision.

Long-distance fares rose Tk 50–100; Selfie Paribahan went Tk 150 → 250; a Bhuiyan Paribahan bus charged Tk 50 where the revised fare was ~Tk 43. Source: Dhaka Tribune, 23 Sep 2026, https://www.dhakatribune.com/bangladesh/dhaka/420525/bus-fare-chaos-hits-commuters-after-fuel-hike

  • Chattogram: minimum bus fares +Tk 3–10; Dhaka–Ctg up to +Tk 100 (Tk 700 → 800 on some services); minibuses +Tk 3–8; CNG tempos on Route 3 Tk 5 → 7. Source: Dhaka Tribune, 25 Sep 2026, https://www.dhakatribune.com/bangladesh/420673/fuel-price-hike-pushes-up-transport-fares-in

2c. Launch fares

  • Launch fares rose 7.54%: +23 paisa/km for the first 100 km, +20 paisa/km beyond, minimum fare Tk 35. Owners had sought +51–60% (Tk 2.95 → 4.45/km for the first 100 km). Announced by Shipping Minister Shaikh Rabiul Alam. Source: Views Bangladesh, 23 Sep 2026, https://viewsbangladesh.com/passenger-launch-fares-hiked-by-754/
  • Operators raised fares before the revised rates took effect. Dhaka–Barishal single cabin went Tk 800–1,000 → 1,200–1,400, deck Tk 250–300 → 350; Dhaka–Bhola Tk 500 → 600; Dhaka–Chandpur Tk 130–150 → 200. BIWTA had "not received official notice" at the time. Source: The Daily Star, 23 Sep 2026, https://www.thedailystar.net/news/transport/news/launch-fares-rise-revised-rates-take-effect-4281286

5. Implications for each study

P1 (fuel-shock panel): W0 timing

  • No new shock landed in the window, and the next scheduled decision point is ~30–31 Oct, when the price for November is announced. The BPC loan makes an October emergency hike less likely. Officials publicly rule out further hikes, but the formula gap has widened (Tk 205 vs Tk 135) and Brent rose in September. Recommendation: keep the 20 Oct W0 completion target. Treat 30 Oct as a hard stop, because the November price notice comes out that day or the next. Record each interview's date so that any respondent after an announcement can be flagged.
  • W0 is now explicitly a post-September-hike, pre-next-shock baseline, not a pre-shock baseline. The fare notification (22 Sep), freight jumps and food price rises have already happened. Add a recall item on fares and food spending "before 21 September" if the aim is to measure the September step.
  • The loan covers ~53 days of diesel losses est., so the next BPC cash crunch plausibly falls in late Nov–Dec. That supports a W1 in early December.
  • Local elections moved to ~January 2027 (EC, 24 Sep). The W0 and W1 windows avoid campaign season, but W2 may not.

P4 (who gets the blame): collection window and events

  • The current window in studies/p4_who_gets_the_blame/src/common.py is "2026": ("2026-03-01", "2026-09-21"). That ends on the hike day and cuts off the entire reaction: protests (21–22 Sep), fare notifications (22–23 Sep), the tax-cut refusal (23 Sep), the BPC loan (29–30 Sep) and the October hold (30 Sep).

P5 (cash or cheap fuel): policy options

  • Duty cut: considered and rejected on fiscal grounds (TBS, 23 Sep). NBR's fuel-tax take is up ~90%. P5 can now frame an explicit "recycle the windfall" option (earmark the ad-valorem windfall for transfers) and a switch from ad-valorem to a specific (per-litre) tax, which experts and the op-ed raised.
  • The government chose a BPC loan, not a subsidy or a price rise. The Tk 4,500 crore is repayable, so the deficit is deferred to BPC's balance sheet. Add "quasi-fiscal deferral" as a status-quo arm in the vignettes.
  • Targeted relief is announced in principle (Zahed, 22 Sep: OMS, Family Card, food assistance) with no reported amount, coverage or date. This makes a good, current anchor for P5's "promised but undelivered" transfer-credibility treatment.
  • The claim "prices will be cut if world prices fall" (State Minister 23 Sep; Home Minister 26 Sep) is now on record. It is useful for P5's asymmetry and credibility questions, and as a claim P4 can track.
